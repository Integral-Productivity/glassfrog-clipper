## Why it exists

Browser extensions are distributed through vendor-run stores. Some of those
stores — Apple's App Store most notably, which is how a Safari Web Extension
reaches users — impose terms on recipients that the GPL forbids: digital
restrictions management, limits on the number of devices, and prohibitions on
redistribution. GPL section 6 says no further restrictions may be imposed on
the rights it grants, so conveying a GPL work through such a store hands the
recipient a licence promising freedoms the channel simultaneously denies them.

That defect is not cured by the copyright holder also being the publisher. It
is a contradiction in what the recipient is offered, not an infringement by
anyone, and it is why VLC was removed from the iOS App Store in 2011.

This permission resolves it in the direction the GPL intends: the store build
is allowed, and the unrestricted source remains available to everyone, so no
recipient is worse off than the GPL would leave them.

## What it does not do

It permits a **distribution channel**. It does not permit a proprietary
derivative.

Every other obligation of the GPL is untouched. Anyone conveying a modified
version must still release the Corresponding Source under the GPL. Combining
this work into a closed-source product still requires a separate licence from
the copyright holder.
